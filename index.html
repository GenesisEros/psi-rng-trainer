How to stay honest

You're both the test subject and the scientist. These rules keep your hopes from bending the results.

  • Decide the count first. Pick 25/50/100/200 before you start. No stopping because it's going well, no quitting because it's going badly. (Ending early is allowed, but it's logged as ABANDONED and still counts.)
  • Pick the target before the draw. The app forces this: DRAW stays locked until a target is chosen.
  • Run control sessions. Same setup, but you don't try. They show what plain luck looks like on your phone.
  • Keep every session. Bad days are data too. Tossing them is how people fool themselves.
  • Compare over weeks, not minutes. One session tells you almost nothing. Look at Intent vs Control, and condition vs condition, after hundreds or thousands of trials.

What the z-score means

The z-score asks: "How far above (or below) pure luck am I, measured in units of normal luck-wobble?"

z = (hits − N×p) ÷ √(N×p×(1−p))

N = number of trials, p = chance of a hit (0.5 for High/Low, 0.25 for 4 symbols). N×p is how many hits luck alone would give you.

  • z between −1 and +1: right at chance. Totally normal.
  • z around +2: interesting. Luck does this only about 1 time in 44.
  • z of +3 or more: strong. Luck does this about 1 time in 740.
  • Negative z: fewer hits than luck. Usually just luck too (some researchers call a steady negative "psi-missing").

Caution: if you run 20 sessions, one of them will probably hit z≈2 by pure luck. That's why a single good session means little. What matters is the cumulative Intent number staying high over many sessions while Control stays near zero. Expect to need thousands of trials before anything is convincing.

The p-value shown is "one-tailed": the chance luck alone would give a score at least this high. Smaller = harder to explain by luck. It uses the standard bell-curve approximation.

About

Each draw uses crypto.getRandomValues, your phone's built-in cryptographic random number generator (the same kind used to make encryption keys). It's seeded from hardware noise inside the device, so it's unpredictable and unbiased; no pattern you could learn or "guess." (It's not a quantum hardware RNG like the PEAR lab used, but it's as close to truly random as a phone gets.)

Draws use "rejection sampling" so every outcome is exactly equally likely.

All data stays on this device in the browser's storage. Nothing is sent anywhere. Export a JSON backup now and then: clearing Safari's website data would erase it.
